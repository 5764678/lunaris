# Thailand's National Space Program and STEM-Education Policy Context for a Lunar-Science Educational Innovation (current to 11 Nov 2026)

Notes compiled 11 Nov 2026. Thai Buddhist Era (BE) years are converted to CE (BE − 543), so 2569 BE = 2026 CE. Labels used: **[DONE]** completed event, **[PLAN]** announced plan or target, **[UNCERTAIN]** sources conflict or are unverified.

## 1. Thailand's lunar and space exploration involvement (Chang'e-7/8, ILRS, Artemis Accords, TSC, Thai lunar plans)

### Takeaway
Thailand is the only country that belongs to both the China-led International Lunar Research Station (ILRS, government-level agreement in April 2024) and the US-led Artemis Accords (signed 16 Dec 2024). Its first lunar payload, NARIT's CE-7 MATCH, is ready and mounted on China's Chang'e-7. That mission, which targets the lunar south pole (Shackleton crater), was due to launch on 24 Aug 2026 but was postponed to 2027, and **it had not launched as of Nov 2026**. A second Thai instrument (CE-8 ALIGN, which looks for south-pole water ice) is set for Chang'e-8 around 2029. In 2026 GISTDA also began pursuing Artemis III/IV participation and a Thai life-science payload on an ispace lunar lander.

### Cited Findings

**ILRS (China-led)**
- [DONE] Thailand's ILRS involvement started in April 2023, when NARIT and Mahidol University proposed a science payload for Chang'e-7. On **25 Sep 2023**, NARIT (under MHESI) and the Deep Space Exploration Laboratory (DSEL) of CNSA signed a cooperation agreement. — [NARIT ILRS page](https://narit.or.th/en/collaboration/ilrs); [NARIT 2023 news (Beijing)](https://old.narit.or.th/index.php/en-news/3552-en20231005-01)
- [DONE] In **April 2024**, Thailand signed a government-level ILRS cooperation agreement and became the ninth ILRS member country. Princess Sirindhorn, CNSA Administrator Zhang Kejian and MHESI Minister Supamas attended. The agreement creates a China–Thailand joint committee and working group for joint space projects. — [SpaceWatch.Global, Apr 2024](https://spacewatch.global/2024/04/thailand-becomes-ninth-country-member-of-the-ilrs-program/); [SpaceNews tag: Thailand](https://spacenews.com/tag/thailand)

**Chang'e-7 / CE-7 MATCH (Thailand's first lunar payload)**
- CE-7 MATCH stands for "Moon-Aiming Thai-Chinese Hodoscope." It is Thailand's first payload designed to orbit the Moon and the first designed and built entirely by Thai scientists and engineers (NARIT with Mahidol University). It is one of seven international scientific payloads on the Chang'e-7 orbiter. It measures high-energy cosmic rays (solar energetic particles, galactic cosmic rays, Jovian electrons, lunar albedo particles) to study the Sun–Earth–Moon system and radiation risks to long-term human presence on the Moon. — [NARIT press release, 23 Aug 2026](https://www.narit.or.th/en/PressRelease-20260823-UPDATE-launch-of-LONG-MARCH-5-Y14)
- [PLAN, then postponed] Launch was set for **24 Aug 2026** on Long March-5 Y14 from Wenchang, Hainan. MATCH was to monitor space conditions and solar storms for three years. — [The Nation, 4 Aug 2026](https://www.nationthailand.com/news/general/40069400)
- [DONE: postponement] On **23 Aug 2026**, NARIT reported a delay of about one month, citing a CNSA official and Typhoon/Storm Narra. — [NARIT, 23 Aug 2026](https://www.narit.or.th/en/PressRelease-20260823-UPDATE-launch-of-LONG-MARCH-5-Y14); [Thai PBS World](https://www.thaipbsworld.com/science-technology/china-postpones-%E2%80%98chang%E2%80%99e-7%E2%80%99-lunar-exploration-launch-by-about-a-month:-narit); [The Nation](https://www.nationthailand.com/news/world/40070182)
- [DONE: postponement to 2027] NARIT's later release on **24 Aug 2026** said the launch was **postponed to 2027**. Chang'e-7 is aiming for **Shackleton crater near the lunar south pole**, and its lighting requirements could not be met in the 2026 window. MATCH "remains ready," has no battery or other time-degrading parts, and can flex on its deployment window. NARIT also named **CE-8 ALIGN** as its next project and mentioned developing a deep-space engineering laboratory. — [NARIT press release, 24 Aug 2026](https://www.narit.or.th/en/PressRelease-20260824-CE-7-MATCH)
- [UNCERTAIN on exact date] Space.com reports Xinhua giving 2027 as the new launch year. As of **9 Oct 2026**, no official Chinese source had given a new date. — [Space.com](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/launches-spacecraft/bad-weather-delays-launch-of-chinas-historic-change-7-mission-to-moons-south-pole); [AZMTH mission tracker](https://azmth.space/moon/missions/change-7); [Aerospace Global News](https://aerospaceglobalnews.com/news/china-delays-change-7-lunar-mission/); [Wikipedia: Chang'e 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang%27e_7) (lists "2027 (planned)")
- Chang'e-7's lander is headed for the rim of Shackleton crater. Its rover will look for subsurface water ice using lunar-penetrating radar. — [Wikipedia: Chang'e 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang'e_7); [Planetary Society](https://www.planetary.org/space-missions/change-7)

**Chang'e-8 / CE-8 ALIGN**

### Inferences
- The timing suits a 2027 award. Chang'e-7, carrying Thailand's first lunar payload, is now expected to launch in **2027**, the same year as the Innovative Award. Its target is the **lunar south pole (Shackleton crater)**, the region LUNARIS already models in terrain and Sun/Earth visibility. CE-8 ALIGN (around 2029) also targets south-pole water ice. LUNARIS can therefore be presented as preparing Thai students to understand the actual science context of Thailand's own lunar instruments.
- Thailand's dual membership in ILRS and the Artemis Accords means a tool using NASA data (LROC/LOLA-type terrain) remains politically neutral and relevant. Thai students will need to understand both the Chinese-led and the US-led south-pole programmes.
- A "Thai lunar orbiter (TSC-2) by 2027" should be described only as a 2021 aspiration. No current source confirms an active Thai-built lunar spacecraft schedule.

## 2. Thai space policy and law (Master Plan 2023–2037, Space Activities Act, space economy targets, GISTDA, National Space Policy Committee)

### Takeaway
The Cabinet approved the National Space Master Plan 2023–2037 on 13 Dec 2022. It has 8 strategies, one of which (Strategy 6) is human-capacity development, but the plan is again under revision in 2025–2026. The Space Activities Act, drafted since around 2017 and Cabinet-approved in draft in 2021, is still **not enacted**. In Aug 2026 the National Space Policy Committee ordered both the Act and the Master Plan reviewed, and made the Artemis programme a key national project. GISTDA's headline space-economy target is THB 300 billion by 2032.

## 3. Thai space-education programmes (NARIT, GISTDA, Space Apps, Space Week, olympiads)

### Takeaway
NARIT runs Thailand's largest astronomy-education system. It has trained teachers continuously since 2010 (over 8,000), lent telescopes to schools in all 77 provinces, held an annual school-astronomy conference (CAAS), and reaches roughly 600k–700k people a year through outreach and regional public observatories. Around the Chang'e-7 launch in Aug 2026, NARIT, MHESI and MOE built a national schools campaign: a live broadcast planned to reach more than 3 million students and a "Thailand CE-7 Moonshot Challenge." That precedent shows government ministries already treat lunar missions as classroom events.

### Cited Findings
- NARIT's **Astronomical Teacher Training and Workshop** has run since **2010** at Basic, Intermediate and Advanced levels. In the Advanced level, students create astronomy projects under NARIT supervision. It has trained **over 8,000 teachers**. — [NARIT teacher page](https://www.narit.or.th/en/public-outreach/teacher)
- NARIT holds an annual **Conference on Astronomical Activities in School (CAAS)** where teachers share activities, attend workshops and receive awards. — [NARIT teacher page](https://www.narit.or.th/en/public-outreach/teacher)
- **Dobsonian telescope loan project:** for about the past 9 years, NARIT has lent 10-inch Dobsonian telescopes free of charge to schools in **all 77 provinces**. Schools must complete a training workshop and submit progress reports, which sustains a teacher network. — [NARIT teacher page](https://www.narit.or.th/en/public-outreach/teacher)
- Astronomy was included in the Thai Basic Education Core Curriculum in **2008**. — [NARIT teacher page](https://www.narit.or.th/en/public-outreach/teacher)
- [UNCERTAIN figures] NARIT web pages variously state outreach of about **700,000 people per year** or "over 600,000 participants annually." ### Inferences
- NARIT's teacher network (over 8,000 trained teachers, telescope schools in all 77 provinces, CAAS) gives an existing channel to pilot and spread LUNARIS. The proposal could frame LUNARIS as a digital extension of NARIT's teacher programme, adding lunar-exploration modules to observation-based astronomy.
- The Aug 2026 MHESI–MOE launch broadcast to more than 3 million students is strong evidence that both ministries treat Thailand's lunar missions as national teaching moments. The rescheduled 2027 Chang'e-7 launch is a natural anchor event for a LUNARIS classroom activity, such as "predict the lighting at Shackleton on launch/landing day."
- Most existing programmes (camps, olympiads, Space Apps) reach small elite cohorts. A free web tool that runs on school devices could reach ordinary classrooms, which is a gap LUNARIS can claim to fill.

## 4. National STEM/education/digital policy that an educational space tool supports

### Takeaway
LUNARIS fits several current policy lines:
- MOE's FY2025–2026 "เรียนดี มีความสุข" (Learn well, be happy) policy.
- The large OBEC "Anywhere Anytime" learning-device and platform programme (about THB 22–30 bn, 29,312 schools, more than 600k rented devices).
- Compulsory computing science since 2018.

## 5. Statements from Thai officials linking space exploration to inspiring youth and STEM careers

### Takeaway
Senior MHESI figures repeatedly frame Thailand's lunar and satellite missions in terms of youth inspiration and human capital. These include Deputy PM/MHESI Minister Yotsanan (Yodchanan) Wongsawat, the MHESI Permanent Secretary, NARIT leadership and GISTDA. In Aug 2026 MHESI explicitly partnered with MOE to put a lunar launch in front of 3 million students.
