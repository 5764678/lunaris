# Presentation video: script and shot list (Thai)

Draft of 21 November 2026. Requirement: landscape, sharp, **at most 3 minutes**, MP4, on
Google Drive with a link anyone can open. This script runs to about **2 minutes 45 seconds**
at a calm speaking pace, which leaves 15 seconds of slack. Don't fill the slack.

## Before recording

- **Record the screen after two things land:**
  - the phone-layout fixes (S-PHONE PR) are merged and deployed;
  - the team's Thai review is done and the "ข้อความภาษาไทยเป็นฉบับร่าง" note is removed from
    `/learn`. A judge seeing "draft" on screen costs you.
- **Screen capture:**
  - Use a desktop browser at 1920×1080, zoomed to 125% so the text reads on a phone.
  - Hide the bookmarks bar and close other tabs.
  - Open each page once before recording; the first load can be slow if the server was asleep.
- **Voice:** two speakers, A and B, alternating. Record the voice separately in a quiet room
  with a phone held about 20 cm from the mouth, then lay it under the screen capture.
- **Captions:** burn in Thai subtitles. Judges often watch with the sound off.
- **Music:** none, or quiet royalty-free music only. Nothing copyrighted.
- **Claims:** don't say anything that isn't in the claims list at the end.

## What to film besides the screen (about 25 seconds of live action)

1. **0:20 and 0:58:** a lamp without a shade, a ball on a stick (styrofoam ball or orange),
   a dark room. Film the ball going around one student's head; the lit half shows the phases.
2. **0:42:** the printed kit fanned out on a table.
3. **1:32:** one worksheet written in by hand. This is an example, not student data, so the
   team fills it in and must not present it as a real student's work.
4. **2:20:** the two of you presenting at a board. Don't stage a "class" and imply it was a
   trial.

## If it runs long

Cut in this order:
1. 1:32–1:40 (period 3), moving its caption onto the 1:12 shot.
2. The second sentence of 2:00–2:20.
3. 0:12–0:20, folding "เรื่องข้างขึ้นข้างแรม" into 0:20.

Never cut the limits (2:40). That line keeps you honest in front of the judges.
