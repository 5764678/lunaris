# Why we built it: in the team's own words

The answers below were written in English by the team lead on 22 November 2026, in reply to
the Technical Director's questions. They are kept as written. The Thai versions used in the
form, poster and video are close translations by the AI assistant. The team reads them aloud
and changes anything that doesn't sound like them.

## The questions and the answers

**1. When you studied the Moon in ม.3, what was hard?**

> It wasn't something that I was STUCK on but it was really inconvenient to get around to. The
> learning process felt way too long to be able to understand. It wasn't crazy hard material
> but I spent quite some time trying to manifest an image in my mind of how the sun and earth
> affected the illumination, and most of it was 2D images.

**2. What made it click?**

> It clicked by just starting to imagine really hard. I learn best when I can understand and
> recreate the process.

**3. Why did you start LUNARIS?**

> I started it for NASA Space Apps [in mind; LUNARIS was not entered there], but for
> education I see a genuine lack of astronomy-related content that is actually kinda cool.
> All the curriculum really teaches is stuff like moon phases, solstices, a bit about the Sun
> and solar storms.

Note from the TD: the ม.3 science indicators (ว 3.1) do include space technology, so the
form says what is defensible instead: the topic is taught mostly through 2D pictures and rarely
connected to real missions or real data.

**4. Who did what?**

> My colleague did a lot of the frontend and giving me feedback. As for me, I did some backend
> and decided and designed some key features (for example the best-site decision, adapted
> from LUNARSEYE).

LUNARSEYE is the team lead's own, separate project, built for NASA Space Apps. Its site-ranking
idea was adapted into LUNARIS's Compare page (see D9 in `docs/DECISIONS.md`). The colleague
also handles contact with the advisor teacher.

## What this means for the design

The problem isn't that phases are hard. It's that a 3D system is taught with flat pictures,
so building the mental model takes a long time. LUNARIS lets a student rebuild and move that
system: step through time, see the Moon from Earth, then stand on the Moon. That is the
principle behind lessons 2 and 4.
